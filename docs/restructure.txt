# soFlott UG --- Website & Business Platform Implementation Brief

**Document type:** Product, website, architecture and implementation
specification\
**Version:** 2.0 --- Revised to make B2B subcontracting a first-class
soFlott Express business model\
**Company:** soFlott UG (haftungsbeschränkt), Germany\
**Audience:** AI coding agents, software engineers, product designers
and implementation partners

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## 1. Purpose of this brief

Redesign and extend the existing soFlott website and business software
into a coherent platform for the company's two commercial divisions:

1.  **soFlott Express** --- courier and logistics services, comprising:
    -   Direct B2B courier deliveries.
    -   B2B subcontracting 
    -   Contracted delivery operations.
2.  **soFlott Digital** --- IT consulting, software engineering and
    related technology services.

The platform must support the full business lifecycle:

**Lead/customer → enquiry → contract/order → service delivery → approved
billable work → invoice → payment → reporting.**

This is an extension of an existing working product, not a greenfield
rewrite.

### Mandatory instruction to the AI coding agent

Before changing code:

1.  Inspect the complete repository, workspace configuration, deployment
    setup, database schema, API routes, authentication and existing
    courier workflows.
2.  Produce a concise inventory of what already exists and identify
    reusable components.
3.  Map existing functionality to the requirements in this brief.
4.  Propose an implementation plan with dependencies, risks and
    migrations before destructive changes.
5.  Preserve working courier functionality and existing customer data.
6.  Implement incrementally, using backward-compatible API changes and
    database migrations.
7.  Add tests and documentation as each capability is introduced.
8.  Do not invent legal, tax, insurance, transport or employment rules.
    Flag unresolved questions for the company and its German tax/legal
    advisers.

Do not replace the existing technology stack merely for stylistic
preference. Do not create duplicate business logic in the new
administration frontend.

## 3. Business models --- required scope

The platform must support three distinct commercial workflows. The first
two are both core parts of soFlott Express.

### 3.1 soFlott Express --- Model A: Direct B2B courier deliveries

**Commercial relationship:** A business customer orders a specific
delivery directly from soFlott.

Typical example: a manufacturer requests collection of a component at
one site and delivery to another business location.

#### Customer journey

1.  Business customer visits the Express website or customer portal.
2.  Customer requests a delivery or asks for a quote.
3.  Customer supplies pickup/delivery addresses, requested date/time
    window and shipment details.
4.  Platform calculates or proposes a price using configured pricing
    rules.
5.  Customer receives a quote or booking confirmation, according to the
    business's approval policy.
6.  Operations accepts and assigns the job.
7.  Driver completes collection and delivery.
8.  Proof of delivery (POD) and exceptions are recorded.
9.  Completed order becomes billable.
10. Invoice is issued individually or included in a consolidated billing
    period.

#### Required capabilities

-   Business customer accounts and multiple contact/address records.
-   Pickup and delivery locations.
-   Shipment description, dimensions, weight and handling instructions
    as relevant.
-   Requested pickup/delivery dates and time windows.
-   Configurable pricing: base fee, distance, urgency, waiting time,
    handling and other approved surcharges.
-   Manual price override with permission and audit history.
-   Quote, acceptance and booking statuses.
-   Dispatch and driver assignment.
-   Delivery status tracking.
-   POD: recipient/name or approved acknowledgement, timestamp, notes
    and optional image/signature where appropriate.
-   Failed delivery, delay, cancellation, damage and other exception
    workflows.
-   Customer notifications.
-   Individual invoice or consolidated periodic invoice.
-   Customer-visible order and invoice history.

Do not assume that a map distance is the contractual billable distance.
Store the agreed price and pricing basis used for the transaction.

### 3.2 soFlott Express --- Model B: B2B subcontracting / contracted delivery operations

**This is a first-class, mandatory business model within soFlott
Express. It must not be omitted, treated as a minor feature of direct
bookings, or deferred as an optional future module.**

**Commercial relationship:** A logistics company, parcel carrier,
distributor or other business contracts soFlott to provide delivery
capacity or execute defined delivery operations. soFlott invoices its
contracting business partner according to the agreement.

This is different from an individual direct B2B delivery order. It has
its own commercial agreement, pricing model, operational planning,
evidence, reconciliation and billing cycle.

#### Typical engagement examples

-   A fixed route operated on agreed weekdays.
-   A defined delivery area or service zone.
-   A daily delivery run with variable parcel volume.
-   Payment per completed parcel or stop.
-   A fixed monthly route fee.
-   A hybrid arrangement combining a base fee and variable volume
    charges.

#### Required subcontracting module

The internal administration navigation must include:

``` text
Express
├── Direct Deliveries
├── Subcontracting
│   ├── Business Partners
│   ├── Contracts & Agreements
│   ├── Contract Rates / Rate Cards
│   ├── Routes & Service Areas
│   ├── Schedules & Working Days
│   ├── Delivery Manifests
│   ├── Driver Assignments
│   ├── Vehicle Assignments
│   ├── Delivery Operations
│   ├── Proof of Delivery
│   ├── Exceptions & Failed Deliveries
│   ├── SLA / Performance
│   ├── Volume Reconciliation
│   ├── Billing Periods
│   └── Subcontract Invoicing
└── Drivers & Vehicles
```

#### Partner and contract management

Store and manage:

-   Contracting business partner and its legal/billing details.
-   Operational contacts and escalation contacts.
-   Contract reference, start/end dates and renewal/termination
    conditions.
-   Contract documents and amendments.
-   Service scope, territory, route definitions and schedules.
-   Operating days, holidays and exceptional service days.
-   Agreed service levels and performance measures.
-   Pricing model and effective-dated rate cards.
-   Agreed treatment of failed, returned, refused or exceptional
    deliveries.
-   Waiting time, additional stops, additional routes and other approved
    surcharges.
-   Invoice frequency, billing cut-off, payment terms and supporting
    statement requirements.
-   Restrictions on further subcontracting, where contractually
    relevant.

Rates must be versioned/effective-dated. A rate change must not silently
alter the calculation for an already completed billing period.

#### Route and delivery operations

Support:

-   Recurring route schedules and ad hoc additional routes.
-   Route/area assignment.
-   Driver and vehicle assignment.
-   Daily manifests or delivery lists, including import where partner
    data is supplied.
-   Unique parcel, consignment, stop or delivery references as
    appropriate.
-   Assignment and dispatch status.
-   Delivery attempt and completion status.
-   POD capture.
-   Failed delivery reason, reattempt, return and exception records.
-   Driver notes and operational incident records.
-   Daily route close-out.

Keep the original imported manifest data or a traceable source record
where useful. Validate imports and identify duplicate or malformed
references.

#### Reconciliation and approval

A delivery manifest or raw scan count must not automatically become an
invoice without validation.

Provide a reconciliation workflow:

1.  Import or create the operational manifest.
2.  Record delivery attempts and outcomes.
3.  Close the route/day.
4.  Compare recorded work against contract rules.
5.  Identify missing PODs, duplicates, disputed items and exceptions.
6.  Apply approved adjustments according to the contract.
7.  Produce a reconciliation statement.
8.  Obtain authorized review/approval.
9.  Freeze the approved billable period or create a controlled
    adjustment.
10. Generate the invoice and supporting statement.

Keep an audit trail of adjustments, reasons, evidence, approver and
timestamps.

#### Subcontract billing models

Support at least:

  Billing model         Calculation concept
  --------------------- ----------------------------------------------------
  Fixed monthly route   Contracted monthly amount
  Per working day       Approved operating days × daily rate
  Per parcel            Eligible completed parcels × agreed rate
  Per stop              Eligible completed stops × agreed rate
  Hybrid                Base fee + approved variable volume/extra services

The exact definition of a billable delivery, stop, attempt or parcel
must be configurable per contract and approved by the business. Do not
hardcode a universal rule.

#### Subcontract reports

Provide operational and financial reporting by:

-   Contracting partner.
-   Contract.
-   Route/service area.
-   Driver/vehicle, subject to privacy and employment requirements.
-   Billing period.
-   Planned versus completed work.
-   Delivery success and exception rates.
-   Reconciled billable volume.
-   Revenue, direct costs and contribution/profitability where cost data
    exists.
-   Outstanding invoices and payment status.

**Important distinction:** subcontracting is a service supplied by
soFlott to its contracting business partner. Do not model it as soFlott
hiring another courier company unless that is a separately configured
arrangement. Support both partner and supplier concepts clearly if the
company later uses further subcontractors.

#### Compliance review prompts

Before activating subcontracting operations, obtain professional review
of the specific business model, including where relevant:

-   German parcel delivery and subcontracting requirements.
-   Social insurance contribution liability and chain-of-subcontracting
    exposure.
-   Driver employment status, payroll and working-time obligations.
-   Insurance coverage for vehicles, goods and liability.
-   Transport permits or licensing requirements for the actual vehicle,
    goods and operating model.
-   Contractual restrictions on further subcontracting.
-   Data protection and permitted handling of recipient information.
-   Cross-border transport considerations if routes cross borders.

The application should support recording required documents, expiry
dates and review status, but must not present itself as determining
legal compliance automatically.

**Commercial relationship:** A business contracts soFlott UG for IT
consulting, software engineering, integration, modernisation or ongoing

Target markets discussed include Germany, the UK, Ireland and the UAE.
Do not imply that the company already has customers or offices in these

Present soFlott Digital as a professional B2B technology supplier, not
merely as an individual job seeker.

Potential services:

-   Custom software development.
-   Full-stack web application engineering.
-   ASP.NET / .NET and modern web application work where supported by
    actual capability.
-   API development and system integration.
-   Legacy application modernisation.
-   Technical consulting and architecture.
-   Ongoing maintenance and support.
-   Contract software engineering.

#### Commercial engagement models

  Engagement                Billing basis
  ------------------------- -------------------------------
  Consulting                Hourly or daily rate
  Time and materials        Approved billable time × rate
  Fixed-price development   Contracted project price
  Milestone project         Approved milestone amounts
  Retainer/support          Recurring monthly fee

#### Required workflow

1.  Create business client and validate legal/billing details.
2.  Record enquiry and proposal.
3.  Create contract and scope.
4.  Create project and milestones or work packages.
5.  Record time, expenses or deliverables as appropriate.
6.  Submit time/expenses for approval where required.
7.  Record milestone acceptance.
8.  Create approved billable items.
9.  Generate invoice according to the contract.
10. Track payment and project profitability.

#### Required features

-   Client and contact management.
-   Proposals/quotes.
-   Contracts and statements of work.
-   Project/work package management.
-   Scope, deliverables and acceptance criteria.
-   Hourly/daily rates and fixed-price schedules.
-   Timesheets and approval.
-   Milestone schedules and acceptance records.
-   Retainers and recurring billing.
-   Change requests and out-of-scope work.
-   Project documents and correspondence references.
-   Invoice history and outstanding balances.
-   Project revenue and cost reporting.

For example, a project could have a 30% commencement payment, 40%
intermediate milestone and 30% final acceptance payment. These
percentages are examples only; make schedules configurable per contract.

International contracting should support clear contract currency,
invoice currency, payment terms, customer country and VAT decision
records. Confirm any currency conversion and accounting treatment with
the accountant.

## 5. Application architecture and user environments

The existing system consists of an **ASP.NET Core C# REST API** and a
**Next.js frontend in one monorepo**, currently handling courier
transactions.

Preserve this stack unless repository inspection identifies a compelling
reason to change it.

### 5.1 Three user-facing environments

1.  **Public website/application:** Marketing, service information,
    enquiries and appropriate public/customer-facing courier functions.
2.  **Customer portal:** Authenticated customers can access their own
    bookings, delivery status, documents, invoices and relevant project
    information as features are introduced.
3.  **Internal administration:** A separate, restricted Next.js
    application for company staff to operate Express, Digital and
    Finance.

The admin application must be a separate frontend deployment and access
boundary. It can reuse the existing backend and database through secured
APIs.

### 5.2 Suggested monorepo structure

Adapt this to the existing repository and package manager. Do not impose
a workspace migration if the current monorepo already has a sound
convention.

soflott-platform/

│   ├── web/                    # Existing Next.js public/customer app
│   ├── admin/                  # New internal Next.js admin app

### 7.2 Billable work as the bridge to finance

**Invoices must be generated from approved billable work and contract
terms, not arbitrary frontend values.**

Examples:

-   Direct Express: completed delivery order at agreed price.
-   Express subcontracting: approved reconciliation statement for a
    contract/billing period.
-   Digital time-and-materials: approved timesheet entries.
-   Digital milestone project: accepted milestone.
-   Digital retainer: contractually due recurring charge.

Billable items should preserve their source references and calculation
details.

### 7.3 Invoice architecture

All divisions use one shared invoice engine and one legal supplier
identity.

Invoice data should include, as applicable:

-   Supplier legal name and address.
-   Customer legal/billing name and address.
-   Invoice number and invoice date.
-   Service/delivery period or supply date.
-   Clear description of services.
-   Quantity, unit, unit price and line amount where relevant.
-   Net amount.
-   Applicable VAT rate and amount, or legally appropriate tax
    treatment.
-   Gross amount where applicable.
-   Currency.
-   Payment terms and due date.
-   Required tax wording and identifiers.
-   Contract/order/project/route references.
-   Supporting statement or attachment references.

Invoice numbering must be unique and consistent. A central sequence is a
reasonable starting recommendation, but numbering policy must be
confirmed with the accountant and implemented with concurrency-safe
allocation.

### 7.4 Issued invoice integrity

Do not silently edit or delete issued invoices.

Provide controlled correction workflows:

1.  Preserve the original issued invoice and its data.
2.  Create a credit note or correction document as appropriate.
3.  Reference the original invoice.
4.  Record reason, user, timestamp and supporting evidence.
5.  Issue the corrected/replacement document where legally appropriate.
6.  Maintain a complete audit trail.

Draft invoices can be edited under controlled permissions; issued
documents require formal correction procedures.

### 7.5 German VAT and international B2B services

The application must not hardcode simplistic rules such as "all UK
invoices are zero VAT" or "all international services are reverse
charge".

Tax treatment depends on the type of service, customer status, country,
place-of-supply rules and applicable exceptions.

For each transaction, retain relevant facts:

-   Customer country and establishment.
-   Business/private customer classification.
-   VAT/tax identification number where applicable.
-   Verification status and date where appropriate.
-   Service category and description.
-   Place-of-supply decision and legal/tax rationale approved by the
    adviser.
-   Tax code/treatment applied.
-   Required invoice wording.
-   Reporting/export classification.

Provide a configurable tax decision framework, but require accountant
review of rules and templates before production use.

Markets discussed include Germany, Ireland, the UK and UAE. The software
should be capable of recording and applying adviser-approved treatments
for these markets. Do not infer that every service supplied to a
customer in one country receives identical treatment.

### 7.6 German e-invoicing readiness

Plan for German domestic B2B e-invoicing requirements and transitional
rules.

-   Support receipt and processing of structured e-invoices.
-   Plan for compliant structured output based on EN 16931 where
    applicable.
-   Evaluate ZUGFeRD and XRechnung support according to customer and
    transaction requirements.
-   Do not assume a normal PDF is a structured e-invoice.
-   Preserve the original structured invoice payload and associated
    rendered view.
-   Validate incoming and outgoing documents.
-   Provide secure export to the accountant/bookkeeping workflow.

Confirm applicable dates, exemptions, formats and obligations with the
current official guidance and tax adviser before release.

### 7.7 Payments and accounting

-   Record payments, partial payments, overpayments and allocations.
-   Track due dates and outstanding balances.
-   Support payment reminders under configurable policies.
-   Distinguish invoice date, service period and cash receipt date.
-   Support accountant-friendly exports and reconciliation.
-   Do not claim the platform is a complete accounting or tax filing
    system unless the required functionality and compliance have
    actually been implemented.

### 7.8 Revenue and profitability reporting

Report by legal entity, division, business model, customer, contract,
route/project and period.

For Express: - Direct delivery revenue and costs. - Subcontract contract
revenue. - Route-level costs and contribution. - Delivery volume and
exception rates. - Outstanding partner invoices.

For Digital: - Consulting revenue by client/project. - Billable versus
non-billable time. - Milestone revenue. - Retainer revenue. - Project
costs and margin where cost data exists.

Do not calculate profitability from revenue alone. Clearly identify
missing cost inputs.

## 8. Administration roles and permissions

Use server-enforced role-based and preferably permission-based
authorization. Roles are collections of permissions, not the only
security check.

Suggested roles:

  -----------------------------------------------------------------------
  Role                                Expected access
  ----------------------------------- -----------------------------------
  Company Owner / Super Admin         All company divisions and settings

  Express Manager                     Express operations and
                                      subcontracting

  Dispatcher                          Delivery scheduling and assignments

  Driver                              Assigned jobs and permitted
                                      POD/operational actions

  Digital Project Manager             Assigned Digital clients/projects

  Finance User                        Invoices, payments, billing and
                                      reports

  Accountant / External Finance       Explicitly granted finance access,
                                      preferably read/export-limited

  Customer                            Own portal data only
  -----------------------------------------------------------------------

Access must be limited by division, record ownership, assignment and
sensitivity where applicable.

Example policy names:

-   `Express.DirectDeliveries.Read`
-   `Express.DirectDeliveries.Manage`
-   `Express.Subcontracting.ManageContracts`
-   `Express.Subcontracting.Reconcile`
-   `Express.Dispatch.Assign`
-   `Digital.Projects.Read`
-   `Digital.Projects.Manage`
-   `Finance.Invoices.Read`
-   `Finance.Invoices.Issue`
-   `Finance.Payments.Manage`
-   `Administration.Users.Manage`

Do not grant every administrator unrestricted finance or user-management
access by default.

## 9. Security, privacy and operational controls

### 9.1 Admin access

-   Separate admin frontend deployment.
-   MFA for all privileged users.
-   Strong identity provider or secure authentication implementation.
-   Consider identity-aware access gateway; VPN/private access may be
    adopted as requirements grow.
-   Short-lived access tokens or secure server-side sessions.
-   Secure refresh-token rotation and revocation if token-based
    authentication is used.
-   Secure, HttpOnly, appropriately configured cookies where applicable.
-   Reauthentication for highly sensitive actions.
-   Session expiry and device/session management.
-   Rate limiting and brute-force protection.

### 9.2 API security

-   Enforce authorization on the backend for every protected endpoint.
-   Never rely on frontend route guards or hidden navigation.
-   CORS is not an authorization mechanism.
-   Protect cookie-authenticated state-changing requests against CSRF.
-   Validate all inputs and file uploads.
-   Apply least privilege to database and service credentials.
-   Protect secrets in deployment secret stores.
-   Avoid logging passwords, tokens, sensitive recipient data or full
    payment details.
-   Apply tenant/record ownership checks even though the platform
    initially serves one legal entity.

### 9.3 Audit trail

Log security-relevant and financially significant events, including:

-   Login and authentication events.
-   Permission and role changes.
-   Customer/billing detail changes.
-   Contract and rate-card changes.
-   Driver/route assignments where material.
-   POD or delivery status corrections.
-   Reconciliation adjustments and approvals.
-   Invoice issue/correction/credit note events.
-   Payment allocation changes.
-   Sensitive document access where appropriate.

Audit records should be tamper-resistant within the system design and
retained according to a reviewed retention policy.

### 9.4 Privacy and GDPR

-   Minimize personal data collected.
-   Limit access to recipient and driver information.
-   Define retention and deletion/archiving policies with legal advice.
-   Record appropriate processing purposes and access.
-   Secure file/document storage.
-   Review processor agreements and hosting locations.
-   Avoid exposing delivery recipient data through public tracking URLs.
-   Ensure portal users can only access records they are entitled to
    see.

### 9.5 Backup and resilience

-   Automated database backups.
-   Protected/off-site backup copies.
-   Backup encryption and access control.
-   Documented recovery objectives.
-   Regular restore tests.
-   Monitoring for failed backups and application errors.
-   Documented incident response and access revocation procedures.

## 12. Testing and quality requirements

### 12.1 Existing functionality regression

Before and after each change, test the current courier transaction
lifecycle. Do not break existing bookings, status transitions, customer
access, pricing or notifications.

### 12.2 Required automated tests

-   Unit tests for pricing, tax-treatment selection inputs, contract
    rate calculations and billing.
-   API integration tests for authorization and business workflows.
-   Frontend component and page tests.
-   End-to-end tests for critical user journeys.
-   Migration tests and rollback/recovery strategy.
-   Import validation and duplicate detection tests.
-   Invoice numbering concurrency tests.
-   Tests proving issued invoices cannot be silently edited/deleted.
-   Permission tests for every privileged API area.
-   Tests proving one customer cannot access another customer's records.
-   Tests for subcontract reconciliation and billing calculations.
-   Tests for timesheet approval and milestone billing.

### 12.3 Key end-to-end acceptance scenarios

**Direct delivery** - A business customer requests a delivery. - The
system calculates a quote using configured rules. - Operations accepts
and assigns the delivery. - Driver completes delivery and records POD. -
The order becomes billable. - An invoice is generated and visible to the
entitled customer.

**Subcontracting** - Admin creates a business partner and subcontract
agreement. - Admin configures a rate card, route and schedule. - A
manifest is imported or created. - Driver is assigned and delivery
outcomes/POD are recorded. - Route/day is closed. - Reconciliation
identifies exceptions and calculates eligible billable work. -
Authorized user approves the billing period. - System generates invoice
and supporting statement. - Revenue and operational performance are
reportable by contract and route.

**Digital consulting** - Admin creates an international business client
and contract. - Project and rate/milestone schedule are configured. -
Consultant records time or milestone completion. - Required
approval/acceptance is recorded. - Approved billable work generates an
invoice using adviser-approved tax configuration. - Payment is tracked.

**Security** - Anonymous visitor cannot access admin. - Authenticated
customer cannot access admin endpoints. - Driver cannot access finance
or Digital projects. - Express manager cannot access restricted Digital
records unless separately authorized. - Finance user cannot change
delivery evidence without permission. - Revoked user/session loses
access.

## 13. Phased implementation roadmap

The sequence below is a recommended delivery plan. The two Express
business models---direct B2B and subcontracting---are both required core
scope. Subcontracting must not be relegated to a speculative later
phase.

### Phase 0 --- Repository and product audit

Tasks: - Inspect monorepo and deployment. - Inventory current Next.js
and ASP.NET functionality. - Map current courier flows and database
entities. - Identify authentication model and API security gaps. -
Inventory current tests and integrations. - Document current routes,
components and shared code. - Identify migration and regression risks.

Deliverable: repository assessment, gap analysis and approved
implementation plan.

Acceptance: - Existing capabilities documented. - No code or data
destructively changed. - Proposed changes mapped to current
architecture.

### Phase 1 --- Brand, public website and information architecture

Tasks: - Establish corporate homepage. - Build Express and Digital
landing pages. - Clearly communicate direct B2B and subcontracting
services separately. - Build enquiry/quote forms. - Add German/English
structure and SEO foundations. - Add legal/trust pages and correct
company identity.

Acceptance: - Visitors can identify the two divisions. - Express
visitors can distinguish direct courier service from logistics
subcontracting. - Subcontracting prospects have a clear proposal/enquiry
path. - Digital prospects have a clear project/contract enquiry path. -
Portal/admin routes are not indexed.

### Phase 2 --- Separate admin application and security foundation

Tasks: - Add separate Next.js admin app in the monorepo. - Reuse
existing API and database. - Implement MFA/secure authentication
approach. - Implement role/permission policies. - Protect API endpoints
server-side. - Add audit logging foundation. - Move or recreate existing
internal courier management screens without disrupting public booking
flows.

Acceptance: - Admin is independently deployed and restricted. - Existing
courier transactions continue to work. - Unauthorized API calls are
rejected. - Roles and permissions are tested.

### Phase 3 --- Express direct B2B workflow

Tasks: - Complete customer/order management. - Configure pricing and
quotes. - Improve dispatch, driver assignment and POD. - Implement
billable-work generation. - Support individual and consolidated
invoicing.

Acceptance: - End-to-end direct delivery scenario passes. - Price
calculation and overrides are traceable. - Completed work can be billed
once and only once.

### Phase 4 --- Express B2B subcontracting (mandatory core Express capability)

Tasks: - Partner management. - Contract and amendment management. -
Effective-dated rate cards. - Routes, areas and schedules. - Manifest
import and validation. - Driver/vehicle assignment. - Delivery outcomes
and POD. - Exceptions and failed delivery workflows. - Route/day
close-out. - Reconciliation, disputes and approval. - Billing periods
and invoice generation. - Contract/route profitability and operational
reports.

Acceptance: - A complete subcontract agreement can be configured. -
Recurring route operations can be scheduled. - Manifest and delivery
outcomes can be captured. - Reconciliation can identify exceptions and
produce approved billable work. - Different billing models calculate
correctly. - Invoice and supporting statement are generated. - Every
adjustment is auditable. - Direct delivery and subcontracting records
remain distinguishable.

### Phase 5 --- Shared finance and compliance foundation

Tasks: - Shared invoice engine and billable item model. - Invoice
numbering and issue controls. - Credit notes/corrections. - Payment
allocation and outstanding balances. - Division and business-model
reporting. - Configurable tax treatment records. - Structured e-invoice
readiness. - Accountant export. - Document archive and retention
controls.

Acceptance: - All three commercial workflows can create billable work. -
Issued invoices are preserved and corrected through controlled
workflows. - Tax treatment is traceable and configurable. - Structured
e-invoice requirements are validated with the accountant before
production use.

### Phase 6 --- Digital consulting and software project management

Tasks: - Digital client and proposal management. - Contracts and
statements of work. - Projects and deliverables. - Timesheets and
approvals. - Milestone acceptance. - Retainers and recurring billing. -
International client tax data and adviser-approved invoice
configuration. - Project revenue and cost reporting.

Acceptance: - Time-and-materials, milestone and retainer workflows
operate. - Approved work generates invoices. - UK, Ireland and UAE
customer scenarios can be configured without hardcoded tax assumptions.

### Phase 7 --- Optimization and future productization

Tasks: - Improve dashboards and management reports. - Add integrations
based on actual business needs. - Improve route/dispatch optimization if
justified. - Evaluate whether parts of the platform could become a SaaS
product for other courier or consulting businesses.

Do not build a multi-tenant SaaS architecture prematurely. First prove
the platform in soFlott's own operations.
