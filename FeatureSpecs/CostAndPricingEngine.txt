# soFlott UG — Centralised Cost & Pricing Management System
## Backend Implementation Specification

**Project:** soFlott UG  
**Module:** Cost & Pricing Management  
**Business Units:** soFlott Express and soFlott Digital  
**Backend:** C# / ASP.NET Core REST API  
**Frontend:** Next.js  
**Database:** PostgreSQL  
**Architecture:** Modular monolith, layered architecture, domain-driven design principles

---

# 1. PURPOSE AND BUSINESS OBJECTIVE

Implement a centralised, dynamic and configurable Cost & Pricing Management System within the soFlott administration backend.

The objective is to enable the company to configure its operational costs, overhead expenses, cost allocation methods, risk reserves, pricing strategies and profit margins centrally.

The pricing engine must use these configurations to automatically calculate sustainable selling prices for all business services.

The system must support:

- Contracted delivery routes.
- Logistics subcontracting.
- On-demand B2B deliveries.
- Recurring B2B delivery agreements.
- Software development.
- API development and integration engineering.
- IT consulting.
- Digital transformation.
- Software maintenance and technical support.
- Other services added in the future.

The system must not contain hardcoded prices or assumptions about operating costs.

All pricing parameters must be configurable through the administration interface.

## Core business principle

Separate the following concepts:

1. Cost configuration: What does it cost soFlott to operate?
2. Cost allocation: How should those costs be distributed across services?
3. Pricing strategy: How should the company charge customers?
4. Price calculation: What is the calculated selling price?
5. Commercial quotation: What price is offered to a customer?
6. Actual profitability: Did the service generate the expected profit?

These must be separate but interconnected domain components.

# 4. COST ALLOCATION ENGINE

Implement a flexible cost allocation engine.

This is one of the most important components.

The engine must distribute fixed and indirect costs to individual services using configurable allocation drivers.

## 4.1 Allocation Methods

Support:

- Per kilometre
- Per hour
- Per working day
- Per delivery
- Per successful delivery
- Per stop
- Per parcel
- Per route
- Per vehicle
- Per employee
- Per project
- Per customer
- Percentage of revenue
- Equal distribution
- Custom allocation driver

Example:

Annual vehicle insurance = €1,200.

Expected annual vehicle operating days = 240.

Insurance allocation per operating day:

€1,200 / 240 = €5.

Another example:

Monthly administration cost = €300.

Expected monthly deliveries = 100.

Administration allocation:

€300 / 100 = €3 per delivery.

The administrator must configure the allocation driver and denominator assumptions.

## 4.2 Cost Pools

Introduce CostPools to group related expenses.

Examples:

Vehicle Operating Cost Pool:
- Fuel
- Tyres
- Brakes
- Maintenance
- Depreciation

Business Overhead Pool:
- Accounting
- Telephone
- Software
- Marketing
- Administration

Digital Delivery Cost Pool:
- Development tools
- Cloud subscriptions
- Infrastructure
- Technical resources

Cost pools must support:

- Multiple cost items
- Business unit restrictions
- Allocation methods
- Effective dates
- Active/inactive status

## 4.3 Avoid double counting

The allocation engine must ensure that one cost is not allocated twice to the same service.

For example, if vehicle insurance is already included in the vehicle operating cost per kilometre, it must not also be independently added through a general overhead allocation.

Implement allocation tracking and validation.

The system must clearly distinguish:

- Original expense.
- Allocated expense.
- Remaining unallocated expense.

# 6. EXPRESS VEHICLE COST CALCULATION

Implement a dedicated vehicle cost calculation component.

A vehicle profile should support:

- Vehicle purchase price
- Purchase date
- Expected residual value
- Expected useful mileage
- Fuel consumption per 100 km
- Current fuel price
- Tyre replacement cost
- Expected tyre lifespan
- Brake replacement cost
- Expected brake lifespan
- Servicing cost
- Expected servicing interval
- Insurance
- Vehicle tax
- Financing or leasing cost
- Other vehicle expenses

Calculate vehicle operating costs dynamically.

Examples:

Fuel cost per kilometre:

(Fuel consumption per 100 km / 100) × Fuel price per litre.

Depreciation per kilometre:

(Purchase price - Expected residual value) / Expected useful kilometres.

Tyre cost per kilometre:

Tyre replacement cost / Expected tyre lifespan in kilometres.

Brake cost per kilometre:

Brake replacement cost / Expected brake lifespan in kilometres.

Service cost per kilometre:

Service cost / Service interval in kilometres.

The total vehicle operating cost per kilometre must combine applicable cost components without double counting.

Also support time-based vehicle costs for vehicles that incur expenses regardless of mileage.

The administrator must be able to change assumptions and immediately preview their impact.

# 8. PRICING STRATEGY ENGINE

Implement a configurable pricing strategy system.

A pricing strategy defines how calculated costs are converted into selling prices.

Support the following strategies.

## 8.1 Cost Plus Pricing

Selling Price = Total Cost × (1 + Markup Percentage).

## 8.2 Target Margin Pricing

Selling Price = Total Cost / (1 - Target Margin Percentage).

Keep markup and margin separate throughout the system.

Never treat them as interchangeable.

## 8.3 Fixed Pricing

Allow an administrator to configure a fixed selling price.

The engine must still calculate the underlying cost and profitability.

Flag prices that fall below configured minimum profitability thresholds.

## 8.4 Unit-Based Pricing

Support pricing by:

- Kilometre
- Hour
- Day
- Delivery
- Successful delivery
- Stop
- Parcel
- Route
- Project
- Month
- Service transaction

## 8.5 Tiered Pricing

Allow different rates based on quantity, distance, duration or volume.

Example:

- First 50 deliveries: Rate A
- Deliveries 51–100: Rate B
- Deliveries above 100: Rate C

Support both progressive and volume-based tiers.

Clearly distinguish between the two calculation methods.

## 8.6 Hybrid Pricing

Support combinations such as:

Base fee + distance rate + waiting time + additional services.

Another example:

Monthly retainer + included support hours + additional hourly charges.

## 8.7 Minimum Charge

Allow a minimum selling price for a service regardless of calculated cost.

## 8.8 Risk and Contingency

Support configurable risk allowances.

Risk allowance methods:

- Fixed amount
- Percentage of direct costs
- Percentage of total costs
- Unit-based reserve
- Custom calculation

Risk allowances must be visible as separate cost components in calculation results.

Do not confuse a risk reserve with an accounting provision or actual expense.

# 11. CENTRAL PRICING CALCULATION ENGINE

Implement a central PricingCalculationService.

Suggested interface:

CalculatePriceAsync(PricingRequest request)

The engine should:

1. Identify business unit and service type.
2. Resolve applicable pricing profile.
3. Resolve applicable cost profile.
4. Load active cost items and allocation rules.
5. Calculate direct costs.
6. Calculate allocated overhead.
7. Calculate vehicle and labour costs when applicable.
8. Calculate project-specific or service-specific costs.
9. Apply risk and contingency allowances.
10. Calculate total estimated cost.
11. Apply the configured pricing strategy.
12. Apply minimum price and pricing constraints.
13. Calculate expected profit and margin.
14. Return a detailed calculation breakdown.

The engine must be deterministic and testable.

Avoid embedding database queries, HTTP requests or UI dependencies inside domain calculation logic.

Use an application service to resolve configuration and a pure domain calculation component to perform the mathematics.

# 12. RATE CARD MANAGEMENT

This is a major feature.

Whenever an administrator creates a B2B contract, contracted route or recurring service agreement, the system should be capable of generating a professional rate card automatically.

A rate card is a customer-facing document defining agreed service prices.

## Rate card generation workflow

1. Administrator creates or selects a customer.
2. Administrator selects the service type.
3. Administrator enters service-specific operational details.
4. System resolves the applicable pricing profile.
5. Pricing engine calculates the cost and recommended selling price.
6. Administrator reviews the calculation.
7. Administrator can adjust the commercial price if authorised.
8. System calculates the resulting profit and margin.
9. Administrator approves the quotation.
10. System generates a rate card PDF.
11. Rate card is attached to the customer contract.
12. Contract receives a snapshot of the approved pricing terms.

## Rate card contents

Include:

- soFlott company information
- Customer information
- Rate card reference
- Contract reference
- Service description
- Pricing unit
- Quantity assumptions
- Agreed unit rates
- Additional charges
- Waiting-time charges
- Overtime charges, where applicable
- Minimum charges
- Volume discounts
- Validity period
- Payment terms
- VAT treatment
- Special commercial conditions
- Document version

The rate card must clearly distinguish net prices, VAT and gross prices where applicable.

## Important

Do not regenerate existing customer rate cards automatically when global pricing configurations change.

Existing contracts must preserve their approved prices.

New pricing configurations should affect new quotations and contracts unless an authorised contract amendment is made.

# 20. VAT AND GERMAN COMMERCIAL REQUIREMENTS

The pricing engine must distinguish:

- Net selling price.
- Applicable VAT rate.
- VAT amount.
- Gross selling price.

VAT must be applied after the net selling price is calculated.

Support configurable VAT treatment by service and tax classification.

Do not hardcode one VAT rate for all services.

The system must allow:

- Standard taxable services.
- Other applicable tax treatments.
- Different VAT classifications where legally required.

Do not assume that every subcontracting arrangement, software service or cross-border B2B service has identical VAT treatment.

Tax treatment must be validated against the applicable German and EU rules.

The pricing engine is not a substitute for tax accounting or invoicing compliance.

# 21. VALIDATION AND BUSINESS RULES

Implement the following protections:

1. No approved price without an applicable pricing profile or explicitly approved manual price.
2. No negative monetary values unless the field specifically permits credits or adjustments.
3. Margin must be between 0% and 100%.
4. Markup and margin must be separately identified.
5. Every calculation must identify its configuration versions.
6. Historical contracts must not change when pricing profiles are updated.
7. Manual price overrides must be audited.
8. A calculation must flag missing cost configuration.
9. Unallocated overhead must be visible.
10. Prevent duplicate allocation of the same cost.
11. Prevent invalid effective-date ranges.
12. Flag prices below minimum profitability thresholds.
13. Distinguish expected delivery volume from guaranteed volume.
14. Distinguish estimated costs from actual expenses.
15. All financial calculations must use decimal arithmetic.

A warning should not automatically block a calculation unless it violates a mandatory business rule.

# 24. ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA

The feature is considered successfully implemented when:

1. An administrator can configure company-wide and business-unit-specific overhead.
2. An administrator can configure vehicle and labour costs.
3. The system can allocate overhead using different cost drivers.
4. An administrator can create different cost profiles.
5. An administrator can create different pricing profiles without code changes.
6. The central pricing engine can calculate different service types.
7. Creating a contracted route automatically generates a recommended price.
8. Creating a subcontracting assignment automatically generates a recommended price.
9. Creating an on-demand delivery automatically calculates its selling price.
10. Creating a Digital service quotation automatically calculates its selling price.
11. The system can generate a customer-facing rate card.
12. Approved contract prices remain historically stable.
13. An administrator can simulate changes to cost assumptions.
14. The system can compare actual and estimated profitability.
15. Every calculation is traceable to its cost and pricing configuration versions.
16. The system prevents silent double counting and exposes unallocated overhead.
17. All calculations are covered by automated tests.

# 25. FINAL ARCHITECTURAL DIRECTIVE

The pricing engine must be service-agnostic at its core but service-aware through configuration and calculation strategies.

Do not build a large collection of hardcoded conditional statements for each service.

Use appropriate strategy interfaces, calculation components and service-specific input models.

Suggested abstractions:

- ICostCalculator
- ICostAllocationStrategy
- IPricingStrategy
- IPricingProfileResolver
- IPricingCalculationService
- IRateCardGenerator
- IProfitabilityAnalyzer

Use a strategy-based architecture where new pricing methods can be introduced without modifying existing service workflows.

The central pricing engine should return a complete, explainable calculation breakdown.

The same calculation must always produce the same result when supplied with the same inputs and the same configuration versions.

Keep the system modular, testable, auditable and extensible.

**Most importantly: the pricing engine calculates the economically sustainable price; the commercial layer determines the final price offered to the customer.**

These are two different responsibilities and must remain separate.
